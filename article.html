Introduction

Every present-day Chinese grapheme is a logogram (a grapheme tied directly to a word or morpheme rather than to an idea or an image). They can still be classified by their original design as pictograms, ideograms or phonosemantograms.

This article deals with phonosemantograms; for pictograms and ideograms see the hieroglyphic procedure. For general background see Wikipedia. The aim of this project is to visualise and measure how far phonosemantograms divide.

Analysing graphemes, and their complexity

Consider a few ways of decomposing them:

  1. not recursive:

    • by stroke count (that is, by continuous strokes)

      the radicals with the most strokes are 龍 'dragon' and 龜 'turtle' at 16, and 龠 'flute' at 17. See this external link

  2. recursive, which can be:

    1. etymological: phonosemantograms are recursive (in their phonetic and semantic components), and so are some ideograms; that is, certain units can enter the composition of larger ones.

      That ideograms are recursive too can be checked without leaving the data: 680 of the 1840 (37 %) appear inside another grapheme, and in 664 of those cases both graphic decompositions agree, the n-ary one and Wikimedia's binary one, which are separate sources. 死 dead; death is one: it sits inside 屍 corpse, 斃 and 毙 to die, 葬 to bury and 薨 death of a prince. The coherence can be read off the definitions; no gloss is needed to point it out.

      NOTE: decomposition proves that the piece is there; it does not prove why. The composition glosses the data ships with, such as «a corpse 死 laid 廾 to rest under the grass 艹», are learner mnemonics, not etymology with an apparatus, and nothing in this article rests on them.

    2. graphic: it follows the division of space, as encoded by the ideographic description characters ⿰ ⿱ ⿲ ⿳ or ⿴ ⿵ ⿶ ⿷ ⿸ ⿹ ⿺ or ⿻

Fonosemagramas

Phonosemantograms are units made of a phonetic component (which encodes the pronunciation) and a semantic one. The semantic component only disambiguates the written sign and has no expression in speech at all (which shows that writing is always more precise than speech); it files the concept under an unsystematic taxonomy of the world, by material for instance: water 氵 for liquids or actions on liquids, wood 木 for furniture among others, hand 扌 for manual actions, and so on.

  • 園 garden, park; orchard yuán
    • 囗 enclosure; border wéi uei˧˥
    • 袁 robe; surname yuán

From here on the colours mark the semantic and phonetic component

NOTE: the class is not homogeneous. Of the 6966 phonosemantograms in the sample, 6760 declare both parts; 157 do not say which is the phonetic one and 49 do not say which is the semantic one. A count by «phonosemantogram» therefore includes 206 units that cannot be decomposed.

NOTE: the radical is not the semantic component. They agree almost always, 6646 out of 6917, but they diverge in 271 cases, and that is where the difference between the two shows: the radical is the dictionary's postal address, the semantic component is an etymological claim. 举 to raise is filed under 丶 and means with 扌 'hand'; 乩 to divine is filed under 乚 and means with 占 'to divine'.


Choosing the phonetic component

  • 鲨 shark shā
    • 沙 sand, gravel, pebbles; granulated shā
      • 氵 water shuǐ
      • 少 few, little; less; inadequate shào
    • 鱼 fish yú

Here a classifier 鱼 for sea animals meets a phonetic component /ʂa˥/ 沙 that supplies the pronunciation of 鲨.

Several logograms shared that pronunciation, however, which raises a question about the design of the grapheme: why was 沙 chosen and not another one that sounds the same? Because the water sub-component 氵 reinforces the meaning? Because of its link with sand 沙?

The sample answers, in three steps.

There was almost never anything to choose between. Sixteen graphemes read shā, but only two ever serve as a phonetic component: 杀 and 沙. And in general, taking as candidates those in the same Karlgren phonetic series that are actually used to write a sound, in 54.6 % of the 1520 measurable cases there was a single candidate.

Reinforcement does not happen. The phonetic component already containing the semantic one, as the 氵 of 沙 does, occurs in 138 of 6760 (2.0 %), and shuffling the phonetic components at random yields 183 (2.7 %): below chance.

Where there was a choice, 690 cases, economy decides.

The piece already in circulation wins, and so does the one written with fewer strokes. Three examples, from the typical one to the one that breaks the rule:

  • 哺 'to chew, to feed' picks 甫, which composes twenty others, over the shorter 父 'father': the used piece wins, not the cheap one.
  • 倚 'to lean on' picks 奇 (composes 17) over 可 (5 strokes, composes 16): two equally common pieces, and neither related to «leaning».
  • 儻 'if perchance' does the opposite of everything: it picks 黨, twenty strokes and no offspring, with 尚 at hand, eight strokes and twenty-three. It is a tendency, not a law.

And meaning, looked at head on, does not motivate the choice either: the phonetic component chosen shares some word of its definition with the grapheme in 2.5 % of cases, and a rival from its own group does so in 1.8 %.

NOTE: both figures come with a catch. «Composes a lot» is partly a consequence of having been chosen, so what is measured is that the system concentrates the work in few pieces. And the meaning test only sees the kinship that surfaces in the definition: it does not see that 停 'to stop' is written with 亭 'pavilion', which is where one stops. The 2.5 % is a floor.


The branch only sounds alike in Old Chinese

Saying that 沙 /ʂa˥/ gives the pronunciation of 鲨 works because Mandarin still preserves it there. Almost never is that so: the phonetic relation is two thousand years old and Mandarin has erased it. Any branch shows it:

  • 各 individual; each, every gè · *kˤak
    • 格 form, pattern, standard gé · *kˤrak
    • 洛 a river in Shanxi luò
      • 落 to fall, to drop luò · *kə.rˤak

In Mandarin these read gè, gé, luò and look like no family at all; in reconstructed Old Chinese they are *kˤak, *kˤrak, *kə.rˤak and the family is beyond dispute. That is why the Baxter-Sagart reconstruction outranks pinyin here: it is the evidence, and the gloss is the commentary. It covers, mind you, only 35 % of the sample, 3359 of 9574, so about two thirds of the graphemes admit no more than «not attested in the classical corpus». The trees are built on this criterion.


Independent verification: Karlgren's series

That a component is the phonetic one is a claim, and it is worth checking against a source other than the one that made it. There is one: the GSR field, the number of Karlgren's phonetic series. If two related graphemes fall in the same series, the relation is confirmed from outside.

Of the checkable edges, 1521 are confirmed (88.1 %) and 205 are disputed (11.9 %). That 12 % is not noise in the way: it is where a reader learns that this is reconstruction and not dogma, and that is why it is marked rather than hidden.

And there is a graver case, with a signature of its own: when the component recorded as semantic falls in the character's series and the one recorded as phonetic falls elsewhere, the two slots are the wrong way round. Of the 854 graphemes where all three carry a series, 13: 勒 協 否 欿 荒 誾 謠 躅 閔 魏 鸞 鼙 齋. In 勒 to tighten, for instance, the data gives 力 as semantic and 革 as phonetic, and Karlgren puts 勒 and 力 in series 0928 and 革 in 0931. One of them is wrong, and the tree of 瀚 at the end of this page shows a case where there is no doubt which.


Semantic recursion

Phonosemantic composition is recursive, as can be seen: a grapheme made of a phonetic and a semantic component can itself be a constituent of another grapheme.

Something uncommon can be seen here: a blue branch that is not terminal

It is uncommon because semantic recursion is very limited next to phonetic recursion; graphemes mostly enter as phonetic components and only a minority, 25 found in the sample, do so as semantic ones.


Maximum divisibility

The maximum level of divisibility observed for phonosemantograms is 4, and it is reached by 4 of them: 礴, 臟, 蘩 and 蠮


  • 蠮 bee yē
    • 虫 insect, worm; mollusk chóng
      • 中 central; center, middle; amidst; to hit (target), to attain; China; Chinese zhòng
    • 翳 shade, screen; to hide, to screen yì
      • 殹 an echo yì
        • 医 to cure, to heal, to treat; doctor; medicine yī
          • 匸 box xì
          • 矢 arrow, dart; to vow, to swear shǐ
        • 殳 tool, weapon shū
      • 羽 feather, plume; wings yǔ

  • 瀚 wide, vast, extensive hàn
    • 氵 water shuǐ
    • 翰 pen, pencil, writing brush hàn
      • 倝 sunrise, dawn gàn
        • 龺 sunlight; the rays of the sun gān
        • 人 man, person; people rén
      • 羽 feather, plume; wings yǔ

NOTE: the data gets the last level of this tree wrong. It gives 倝 龺 as its semantic component and 人 as its phonetic one, and it is the other way round. Old Chinese settles it: 倝 *[k]ˤar-s, 幹 *[k]ˤar-s and 翰 *m-kˤar-s fall in the same Karlgren phonetic series, 0140, and 干 in the adjacent one; 人 *ni[ŋ] is in 0388 and shares with them neither onset, nor rhyme, nor coda. The sound comes from the 干/龺 side and 人 is a stroke. The source itself confirms it by another route: its hint for 倝 reads only sunlight, which describes 龺, and the one for 幹 reads Sunlight 龺 suggests "dry"; a club 人干 suggests "invade", where 人 is a drawn club and not a sound.

The tree is left as it stands because it is drawn from the data, and correcting it by hand would depart from the source without saying so. The case is not unique: there are thirteen graphemes in which the component recorded as semantic is precisely the one that sounds alike, and the one recorded as phonetic is not. It is measured in the sources.


The other recursion, the graphic one

The 4 above is etymological divisibility. The graphic one is still owed, announced at the start as a different way of decomposing; it reaches much further, because it counts strokes inside strokes and not meanings inside meanings. There are three measures and they should not be confused:

The case that separates them is 躑 to hesitate: 7 levels if it is always split in two, 8 if splitting in three or more is allowed, and only 1 etymologically: it splits once, into 足 'foot' and 鄭, and that is the end of it. Saying «divisibility» without saying which of the three is the shortest road to two correct measurements contradicting each other: it happened in this project's own notes, where one said 7 and another said 8 of the same character.
